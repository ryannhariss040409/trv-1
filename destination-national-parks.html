National Parks Road Trip

Zion, Bryce, Capitol Reef, Arches and Canyonlands sit inside a 400-mile arc. Done properly it is nine days. Done badly it is nine days of driving with parking lots in between.

Plan a parks road trip See the 9-day itinerary

Overview

Southern Utah holds five national parks close enough to string together in a single loop, which is why this is the most requested domestic road trip we plan. The parks are genuinely different from one another: Zion is a narrow canyon you walk up into, Bryce is an amphitheater of hoodoos you walk down into, Capitol Reef is a hundred-mile geological wrinkle almost nobody stops at, Arches is a compact drive-and-short-hike park, and Canyonlands is enormous, empty and split into districts that do not connect.

Nine days is the number that works. Seven is possible if you drop Capitol Reef. Five turns the trip into a windshield tour, and we would honestly rather you did two parks properly than five badly. The loop typically starts and ends in Las Vegas, which usually has cheaper flights and cheaper rental cars than Salt Lake City, though SLC is the better choice if you are adding Grand Teton or Yellowstone.

This trip suits families with kids over about six, couples who like being outside, and anyone who has never done a US road trip and wants a first one that is impossible to mess up navigationally. It does not suit anyone who needs a pool and a cocktail at 4:00 PM, and it is genuinely hard in July and August heat.

On permits and timed entry. Requirements change year to year. Zion has used a lottery for Angels Landing since 2022. Arches has run a timed-entry reservation system during peak months. Both are managed through the federal recreation booking system and both sell out. Check the official National Park Service page for each park roughly three months before your trip and again two weeks out. Do not rely on a blog post, including this one, for the current rules.

Buy the America the Beautiful pass. It costs $80 for a year and covers entry to every federal recreation site for everyone in one vehicle. Individual park entry is $30 to $35 per vehicle, so it pays for itself at the third park. Buy it at the first entrance station you reach.

The largest controllable cost is lodging, and it varies enormously by town. Springdale at the Zion gate is the most expensive stop on the route. Hurricane or La Verkin, twenty minutes west, can be 40 percent cheaper for the same night. Moab in April and May is close to Springdale pricing and books out months ahead.

Two costs people forget: a cooler and its ice, which you will refill every other day, and the one-way rental drop fee if you fly into Las Vegas and out of Salt Lake City. That fee is often $150 to $300 and can erase the airfare saving that made the open jaw attractive.

Getting around

You need a car, and a standard sedan or small SUV is enough for every paved route in this guide. High clearance and four-wheel drive matter only for specific unpaved roads such as Canyonlands' White Rim or Capitol Reef's Cathedral Valley loop. Rental agreements usually prohibit driving those anyway.

Zion Canyon is shuttle-only for most of the year. You park in Springdale or at the visitor center and ride in. There is no way around this and there is no point trying.

Distances between parks: Zion to Bryce is 86 miles, Bryce to Capitol Reef is 111 miles, Capitol Reef to Moab is 145 miles, Moab to Las Vegas is 460 miles. Add 20 percent to any mapping estimate, because you will stop.

Fuel up at half a tank. On Highway 12 and Highway 24 the gaps between stations run 60 to 70 miles and some close at 8:00 PM.

Cell coverage is unreliable in all five parks. Download offline maps for the entire region before you leave Las Vegas.

Where to stay

Springdale, 2 nights. At the Zion gate, walkable, expensive. $190 to $340 in season. Hurricane and La Verkin are 20 to 30 minutes out and considerably cheaper.

Bryce Canyon City or Tropic, 1 to 2 nights. Tropic is smaller, cheaper and 15 minutes from the rim. $120 to $210.

Torrey, 1 night. The base for Capitol Reef. A handful of good small lodges and one very good restaurant. $130 to $230.

Moab, 3 nights. Covers both Arches and Canyonlands with a rest day. $150 to $290, higher during the spring events season.

In-park lodging exists at Zion Lodge and Bryce Canyon Lodge. Both release rooms roughly 13 months ahead and both are gone within days. If you want one, set a calendar reminder rather than hoping.

Safety notes

The desert is not dangerous in a dramatic way. It is dangerous in a boring, cumulative way: heat plus exertion plus not enough water plus an overestimate of your own fitness.

  • Carry one gallon of water per person per day in summer, and more if you are hiking. Search and rescue calls in these parks are overwhelmingly heat and dehydration cases, not falls.
  • Flash floods are the real killer in slot canyons. A storm 30 miles away can flood a canyon under a blue sky above you. Check the flash flood potential rating at the Zion visitor center every morning before entering the Narrows.
  • Angels Landing has a chain section with severe exposure and has caused fatalities. The permit lottery exists for crowd control, not to certify that you should go. If heights bother you, the Watchman Trail gives you a view without the ridge.
  • Afternoon thunderstorms in July and August arrive fast. Get off exposed rims and off slickrock before 2:00 PM in monsoon season.
  • Do not step on cryptobiotic soil. The dark crusty ground between plants is a living organism that takes decades to recover. Stay on rock or in established washes.
  • Tell someone your plan and expect no cell signal. A paper map in the glovebox is not paranoid here.

Wildlife is a minor concern. Rattlesnakes exist and want nothing to do with you. Do not feed anything, and keep food inside the car overnight in Moab, where ravens are genuinely skilled thieves.

Check current conditions at the official National Park Service page for each park before you drive. Road and trail closures are common after storms. See our Disclaimer for how we handle safety information.
