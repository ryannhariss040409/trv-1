Accessibility Statement

Last updated 08/01/2026. Owned by Imani Castellanos, Head of Support and Accessibility.

1. Our commitment

Driftline Travel Co. is committed to making www.driftline.travel usable by everyone, including people who use screen readers, keyboard-only navigation, screen magnification, voice control, or who need reduced motion or higher contrast.

We measure ourselves against the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 Level AA. We believe the site currently conforms with WCAG 2.1 Level AA, with the known exceptions listed in section 4. We treat an accessibility barrier as a bug, not a feature request, and we prioritize it accordingly.

2. What we have implemented

  • Semantic structure. Every page uses proper landmarks: a single main element, labeled nav regions, header and footer, and a logical heading order starting at one level-one heading per page.
  • Skip to content. The first focusable element on every page is a skip link that jumps directly to the main content.
  • Keyboard operation. Everything on the site can be reached and operated with a keyboard alone, including the mobile menu, the More dropdown, FAQ accordions, filter controls, the cookie preferences dialog and the trip planner.
  • Visible focus. Focus indicators are never removed. Focused elements receive a three pixel outline in our accent color with a clear offset, which meets the WCAG 2.1 non-text contrast requirement against every background we use.
  • Color contrast. Body text meets or exceeds a 4.5 to 1 contrast ratio against its background. Large text and interface components meet or exceed 3 to 1. Our translucent glass panels are calibrated so that text over them stays above the 4.5 to 1 threshold rather than drifting toward the background gradient.
  • Color is never the only signal. Form errors show an icon-free text message below the field, a border change and an aria-invalid attribute, not just a red outline.
  • Images. Every content image has descriptive alternative text. Decorative images, including the background gradient and hero blur layers, are marked aria-hidden or given empty alt text so screen readers skip them.
  • Reduced motion. Every animation on the site, including the drifting background gradient, panel hover lifts and scroll reveals, is disabled under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce. Content is never hidden behind an animation that could fail to run.
  • Reduced transparency. Under prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce, glass panels fall back to a near-solid background.
  • Touch targets. Interactive controls are at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels with spacing between them.
  • Text resizing. The layout reflows without loss of content or horizontal scrolling at 200 percent zoom and at a 320 pixel viewport width. We do not disable pinch zoom.
  • Live regions. Totals in the planner and budget estimator, and form status messages, are announced through polite live regions so screen reader users hear updates without losing their place.
  • Tables. Data tables use caption, thead and scoped header cells so the relationship between a figure and its label is available to assistive technology.
  • Language. Every page declares lang="en".

3. How we test

Before a page ships we run through this list:

  • A full keyboard pass with no mouse, checking focus order and that nothing traps focus.
  • An automated audit using an accessibility engine built into browser developer tools.
  • A screen reader pass using VoiceOver on macOS and NVDA on Windows.
  • A contrast check on every text and background pairing, including text over translucent panels.
  • A reduced-motion pass with the operating system preference switched on.
  • Layout checks at 320, 360, 768, 1024, 1440 and 1920 pixel widths, and at 200 percent browser zoom.

Automated tools catch roughly a third of real issues, so manual testing is the part that matters. We repeat the full pass at least twice a year across the whole site.

4. Known limitations

We would rather list these than claim perfection.

  • Third-party advertising. Advertisements are rendered by external networks and their markup is outside our control. Some ad creatives may not meet WCAG requirements. We isolate ad slots in labeled regions so they can be skipped, and we do not place them in the main reading order.
  • PDF exports. Our offline PDF export produces a tagged document, but complex multi-day itineraries may have imperfect reading order in some PDF readers. Work on this is scheduled for the fourth quarter of 2026. In the meantime, the live share link is fully accessible and we will produce an alternative format on request.
  • Older reader guides. A small number of guides published before 2023 contain tables that predate our current header markup. We are updating them as each guide comes up for its twice-yearly refresh.
  • Photography descriptions. Alt text describes what a photograph shows, but our images are decorative context rather than information carriers, so descriptions are brief by design. Nothing essential is conveyed by an image alone.

5. Compatibility

The site is designed to work with recent versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, on Windows, macOS, iOS and Android, in combination with NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver and TalkBack. It should degrade gracefully in older browsers: with JavaScript disabled, all content remains readable and every navigation link still works, though the planner and cookie preferences require JavaScript to function.

6. Reporting a barrier

If any part of this site is difficult or impossible for you to use, tell us. You do not need to know why it is broken or use any particular terminology. Describing what you were trying to do and what happened is enough.

  • Email: hello@driftline.travel with "Accessibility" in the subject line
  • Phone: +1 (310) 555-0147, Monday to Friday, 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM Pacific
  • Contact form: select "An accessibility problem" on the contact page
  • Mail: Accessibility, Driftline Travel Co., 1201 Ocean Avenue, Suite 410, Santa Monica, CA 90401

Our response commitment: we acknowledge every accessibility report within 2 business days and give you a plan with a date within 10 business days. Barriers that block someone from completing a core task, such as building or reading a trip plan, are treated as urgent and fixed ahead of feature work.

7. Alternative formats

If you cannot use a page on this site, we will provide the same information another way at no charge. That includes a plain-text version of any guide or itinerary, a large-print version, or a phone call in which a member of our team reads a plan to you. Ask using any of the routes above.

8. Formal complaints

If you are not satisfied with our response, you may escalate by writing to the mailing address above marked for the attention of the Head of Support and Accessibility. We will review the matter and respond in writing within 20 business days.

This statement was prepared on 08/01/2026 based on a self-evaluation carried out by Driftline Travel Co. It is reviewed at least annually and whenever a significant change is made to the site.
