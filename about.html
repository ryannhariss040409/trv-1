About Driftline

We are a fourteen-person company in Santa Monica, California, building one thing: a planner that turns travel ideas into a real schedule with a real number attached.

Why this exists

Driftline started in 2021 out of a specific frustration. Our founder had spent eleven years managing corporate travel, which meant watching capable adults with real budgets produce trips that fell apart on day three. The pattern was always the same. Enormous research, dozens of saved links, and no schedule. Nobody had ever written down what happens at 2:00 PM on Tuesday.

The tools available split into two camps. Booking sites wanted you to buy something immediately. Note apps let you collect ideas forever without ever committing to a day. Nothing sat in the middle and asked the boring question: what time, how long, how much, and how do you get to the next thing.

That question is the entire product. Every feature we have built since is a version of it.

What we believe about trip planning

Time is the real constraint, not money. Most American travelers are limited by PTO days rather than budget. A planner that ignores this produces beautiful, impossible schedules.

Travel time between stops is the most useful number on a page. It is the thing that reveals an overloaded day before you are standing on a corner at 4:00 PM realizing you cannot make the reservation.

An honest estimate beats a precise-looking guess. We publish cost bands, not live prices, because live prices for a trip four months away are theater. A band you can plan against is more useful than a number that will be wrong.

Nobody should have to fight software to leave. The free tier does not expire, downgrading does not delete your work, and cancelling takes two clicks with no retention call.

Editorial standards

Everything in a destination guide is either something a member of our team has done personally or something verified against an official source such as a national park service, a transit authority, or a government tourism board. When a guide relies on a source we could not verify ourselves, we say so in the text.

  • We do not accept payment for placement inside a guide. There is no price at which a hotel can appear in a where-to-stay section.
  • Affiliate links exist, they are labeled at the point of use, and they never change the order of a recommendation. Details are in our Affiliate Disclosure.
  • Cost bands are re-checked at least twice a year, and the update date appears at the top of every guide.
  • Reviews on our testimonials page come from a post-trip survey, are published with permission, and are not edited for wording. We do not buy reviews and we do not write them ourselves.
  • When we get something wrong, we correct the page and note what changed rather than quietly editing it.

How we make money

We say this on the homepage too, because a planning tool that recommends places has an obvious conflict of interest and pretending otherwise helps nobody.

  1. Pro subscriptions at $9 per month or $84 per year. This is the largest share of revenue and the part we want to grow, because subscriber money is the only money not tied to you booking something.
  2. Affiliate commissions from some hotel, flight, car rental and tour links. The price you pay does not change.
  3. The custom itinerary service, a one-time fee starting at $149 for a human-reviewed plan.
  4. Agent and white-label plans for travel advisors and small tour operators, $49 and $199 per month.
  5. Advertising in clearly labeled slots that sit below the main content and never appear on legal pages.
  6. Travel insurance referrals, which pay us a referral fee if you buy a policy and are entirely optional.

We have taken no venture capital. Fourteen people, funded by subscriptions and the rest of the list above.
