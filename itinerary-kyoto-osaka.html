Kyoto and Osaka in six days

Four days in Kyoto routed one district at a time, a Nara day trip, and two nights in Osaka. No rental car, no rail pass, no doubling back across the city.

Before you start

The core idea of this plan is one district per day. Kyoto's sights are spread around a ring, and crossing that ring mid-day costs 45 to 70 minutes on a bus. Most first-time itineraries zigzag, which is why so many people come home describing Kyoto as exhausting.

  • Skip the nationwide rail pass. For a Kansai-only trip it does not pay for itself. Get an IC card at the airport, load $40, top up as needed.
  • Book first: lodging in Kyoto, particularly for late March through early April and mid-November. Good mid-range rooms disappear four to six months ahead in those windows.
  • Start early, genuinely early. Two of the six days begin before 7:00 AM. That is not enthusiasm, it is the only way to see Fushimi Inari and the Arashiyama bamboo grove without a crowd.
  • Carry cash. Card acceptance keeps improving but temple admissions, small restaurants and some buses remain cash only. 7-Eleven ATMs accept US cards reliably.

Costs below are per person and exclude international airfare. Full breakdown in the Kyoto budget table.

The six days

Day 1

Southern Higashiyama, on foot

Est. $78 / person
  • 07:15 AMKiyomizu-dera, $3

    Opens at 6:00 AM. Arriving before 8:00 AM is the difference between a temple and a turnstile.

    → 12 min walk downhill
  • 09:00 AMSannenzaka and Ninenzaka

    Stone-paved lanes of wooden shopfronts. Empty at this hour, shoulder to shoulder by 11:00 AM.

    → 15 min walk
  • 10:30 AMKodai-ji temple, $4

    Consistently skipped by visitors and consistently one of the best gardens in the district.

    → 10 min walk
  • 12:30 PMLunch near Yasaka Shrine, $14

    Then an hour in Maruyama Park doing nothing at all, which day one needs after a long flight.

    → 8 min walk
  • 03:00 PMChion-in temple, free grounds

    The main gate is the largest wooden gate in Japan. Twenty minutes, and almost nobody is there.

    → 15 min walk
  • 05:00 PMGion at dusk, dinner $32

    Walk Hanamikoji. Photography is restricted on the private side lanes and the rules are enforced with fines.

Walking: 6.2 milesTransit: 2 ridesPace: moderate
Day 2

Arashiyama and the west

Est. $96 / person
  • 07:30 AMJR Sagano line to Saga-Arashiyama, $3

    Twenty minutes from Kyoto Station. There is no version of this day that works starting at 10:00 AM.

    → 10 min walk
  • 08:15 AMBamboo grove, free

    Open all hours. Completely different before 9:00 AM. Twenty minutes is enough.

    → 6 min walk
  • 09:00 AMTenryu-ji garden, $6

    The pond garden dates to the 14th century and is the real reason to be in this district.

    → 20 min walk uphill
  • 11:00 AMIwatayama Monkey Park, $5

    A genuine 20 minute climb. Skip it if anyone in the group is unsteady on stairs.

    → 25 min walk and train
  • 01:00 PMLunch by the Togetsukyo bridge, $16

    Tourist-priced but pleasant. Cheaper options are two streets back from the river.

    → 45 min bus
  • 02:30 PMKinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion, $4

    Thirty minutes end to end. It is one view, it is always crowded, and it is still worth doing once.

Walking: 5.4 milesTransit: 4 ridesPace: full
Day 3

Fushimi Inari, then Nara

Est. $104 / person
  • 06:45 AMFushimi Inari Taisha, free

    Open 24 hours. At 7:00 AM the gate tunnels are nearly empty. By 10:00 AM they are a queue.

    → 45 min hike up and back
  • 09:30 AMJR Nara line from Inari, $5

    About 45 minutes. Buy a bento at the station rather than eating in Nara at noon prices.

    → 20 min walk
  • 11:00 AMTodai-ji and the Great Buddha, $5

    One of the largest wooden buildings anywhere, holding a 49-foot bronze Buddha.

    → 15 min walk
  • 01:00 PMNara Park and the deer, crackers $2

    They bow for food and they will also headbutt you. Genuinely fun, mildly chaotic, great with kids.

    → 25 min walk
  • 03:00 PMKasuga Taisha lantern paths, $4

    Three thousand stone lanterns along a cedar-shaded approach. Quieter than everything before it.

    → 50 min train back
  • 06:00 PMDinner on Pontocho alley, $38

    Nishiki Market closes around 6:00 PM, so this is a Pontocho night if Nara ran late.

Walking: 7.8 milesTransit: 4 ridesPace: full
Day 4

Central and northern Kyoto

Est. $88 / person
  • 09:00 AMNijo Castle, $8

    The nightingale floors squeak deliberately as an intruder alarm. Ninety minutes including the garden.

    → 20 min subway
  • 11:15 AMRyoan-ji rock garden, $4

    Fifteen rocks in raked gravel, and you can only ever see fourteen at once. Sit down and stay a while.

    → 25 min bus
  • 01:00 PMNishiki Market lunch, $18

    Five covered blocks of food stalls. Eat standing where you buy, which is the local etiquette.

    → 10 min walk
  • 03:00 PMPhilosopher's Path, free

    A canal-side walk of about 1.2 miles ending at Ginkaku-ji. Cherry trees the whole way.

    → 5 min walk
  • 04:30 PMGinkaku-ji, the Silver Pavilion, $4

    Never actually silvered. The moss garden and the sand cone are the point.

    → 30 min bus
  • 07:00 PMIzakaya dinner near Karasuma, $34

    Last Kyoto night. Repack tonight, because tomorrow starts with a hotel change.

Walking: 6.6 milesTransit: 5 ridesPace: moderate
Day 5

Move to Osaka, Namba and Dotonbori

Est. $112 / person
  • 09:30 AMCheck out, train to Osaka, $6

    Roughly 30 minutes on the JR Special Rapid. Drop bags at the Osaka hotel or in a station locker.

    → 20 min subway
  • 11:00 AMShitennoji temple, $3

    Founded in 593, which makes it one of the oldest Buddhist temples in Japan. Quiet, and rarely on a first-timer list.

    → 15 min subway
  • 01:00 PMKuromon Ichiba Market lunch, $22

    Osaka's kitchen. Grilled scallops, wagyu skewers, fruit that costs more than you expect.

    → 10 min walk
  • 03:00 PMDen Den Town or Amerikamura

    Electronics and anime in one, vintage clothing and street art in the other. Pick by taste.

    → 15 min walk
  • 06:30 PMDotonbori at night, dinner $36

    Neon, canal, and the food that Osaka is actually famous for. Takoyaki and okonomiyaki, eaten standing up.

Walking: 5.9 milesTransit: 5 ridesPace: moderate
Day 6

Osaka Castle, Umeda and departure

Est. $84 / person
  • 08:30 AMOsaka Castle park and keep, $6

    The keep is a 1931 reconstruction with an elevator and a museum inside. The park around it is the better part.

    → 25 min subway
  • 11:00 AMUmeda Sky Building observatory, $12

    Open-air floating garden on the 39th floor. Better than the enclosed alternatives and less busy in the morning.

    → 10 min walk
  • 12:30 PMLunch in Umeda, $18

    The department store basements have some of the best cheap food in the city.

    → 20 min walk to the hotel
  • 02:30 PMCollect bags, train to KIX, $16

    Allow 75 minutes to the airport. The Haruka limited express is the fastest option.

    → 75 min train
  • 05:00 PMCheck in for the US-bound flight

    Most departures to the West Coast leave in the late afternoon and land the same calendar day.

Walking: 4.8 milesTransit: 4 ridesPace: easy

How to adapt this

Adding Tokyo? Then the nationwide rail pass arithmetic changes and may work in your favor. Fly into Tokyo Haneda, out of Osaka Kansai, and run the trip one direction rather than backtracking.

Only have four days? Drop Osaka entirely and keep days one through four. Osaka is a good city but it is not why anyone flies to Kansai for the first time.

Traveling with kids? Swap day four for a Universal Studios Japan day in Osaka, and move the Nara deer day earlier. Both are reliably the highlights for children under twelve.

Going in November? Add 45 minutes to every temple visit. Fall color draws domestic crowds and the popular gardens run evening illumination sessions that need separate tickets.
