The whole method, no mystery

Four steps between a vague idea and a real plan

Most trip planning fails at the same place: you collect ideas but never assign them to a specific hour of a specific day. Driftline is built around that one problem.

Open the planner Browse finished examples

The four steps

Step one

Set the shape of the trip

Enter the destination, your travel window and the number of days you can genuinely take. We ask for PTO days rather than calendar days on purpose, because the two are rarely the same. A Saturday departure and a Sunday return turns five vacation days into nine days on the ground.

If you are flying internationally, add your departure airport. The planner treats the first and last day differently: a red-eye out of Newark that lands in Reykjavik at 6:30 AM is not a full day, and neither is a day that ends at an airport three hours before boarding.

Step two

Fill the days with real stops

Pull stops from one of our destination guides, or type your own. Every stop takes four things: a start time, a name, a cost in USD, and the travel time to whatever comes next. That last field is the one that saves trips.

You do not need to know the exact minute. A stop that says 02:30 PM, Museum, $26, 20 min transit is enough to reveal that your afternoon has four hours of content and two and a half hours of daylight.

Step three

Balance the pace against the budget

As you build, the day card shows a daily cost per person and the total distance covered. The trip summary adds it up across every traveler in the party. When the number is uncomfortable, you can see precisely which day is doing the damage.

In our experience, the fix is almost never cheaper hotels. It is usually one fewer paid attraction per day and one more long lunch, which also happens to make the trip better.

Step four

Take it with you

Share a link with the people you are traveling with so everyone edits the same plan instead of three group chats. Pro subscribers can export an offline PDF, which matters more than it sounds when you are in a canyon with no signal and a 2:00 PM permit window.

Free plans can print the page from the browser, which produces a clean paper copy with the ad slots and navigation removed.

How we make money

A planning tool that recommends places has an obvious conflict of interest. Here is ours, written out.

Subscriptions come first

Pro subscriptions are the largest share of our revenue and the part we want to grow. Subscriber money is the only money that is not tied to you booking something, which is why it funds the guides.

$9 per month, or $84 billed once per year.

Affiliate commissions

Some hotel, flight, car rental and tour links on this site are affiliate links. If you book through one, the partner pays us a commission at no additional cost to you. Those links carry a visible label at the point of use.

We do not accept payment for placement inside a guide, and a commission never changes the order of our recommendations. Full disclosure.

Custom itinerary service

If you would rather hand the work to a person, our team builds a reviewed plan for a one-time fee: $149 for trips up to seven days, $249 for eight to fourteen days. You get two revision rounds and a call if you want one.

This is a service fee, not a booking fee. We do not book anything on your behalf.

Agent and white-label plans
