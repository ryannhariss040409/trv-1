Iceland in seven days

Reykjavik, the Golden Circle, the south coast to Jokulsarlon, then back west for the Snaefellsnes peninsula. Built for one week of PTO with a weekend on each end.

Before you start

This route is an out-and-back along the south coast rather than a full Ring Road loop. That is deliberate. The complete circuit is 828 miles of Route 1 alone, and squeezing it into seven days means four to six hours of driving on most days. What follows keeps every driving day under three and a half hours except day five.

  • Book first: the rental car and the Blue Lagoon time slot. Both sell out in summer, the car often before the lagoon.
  • Book second: the Vik and Jokulsarlon-area guesthouses. Options are limited and disappear by March for a July trip.
  • Do not book: anything weather-dependent more than a week ahead. Glacier walks and aurora tours can be rescheduled cheaply if you wait.
  • Pack: waterproof shell and pants, warm mid-layer, hat and gloves in every month, swimsuit, and a reusable water bottle. Tap water is excellent and free.

Daily costs below are per person, assuming two travelers sharing a car and a room. They exclude airfare and the rental car itself, which are listed separately in the budget table.

The seven days

Day 1

Land, soak, sleep early

Est. $186 / person
  • 06:30 AMArrive at Keflavik International

    Red-eyes from Boston, Newark and Chicago land before sunrise. Collect the car first, then passport control is usually quicker.

    → 20 min drive
  • 09:00 AMBlue Lagoon, first entry slot, $75

    Books out weeks ahead. It works as a shower after the flight and it resets jet lag better than a hotel nap ever does.

    → 50 min drive
  • 01:00 PMLunch on Laugavegur, Reykjavik, $28

    Lamb soup and a flat white. Then a Kronan or Bonus run for the cooler, which is the single biggest money saver of the week.

    → 10 min walk
  • 04:30 PMHallgrimskirkja tower, $9

    Best overview of the city and it takes twenty minutes. Then walk down to the Sun Voyager sculpture for sunset.

    → 15 min walk
  • 07:30 PMEarly dinner and an early night, $52

    Do not fight the jet lag with a late night. Day two starts at 8:00 AM.

Driving: 82 milesWalking: 2.4 milesPace: easy
Day 2

Golden Circle, slowly

Est. $154 / person
  • 08:00 AMFuel up and leave Reykjavik

    Town fuel is meaningfully cheaper than rural pumps. Fill completely, not partially.

    → 45 min drive
  • 09:00 AMThingvellir National Park, $6 parking

    Walk the Almannagja rift before the tour coaches arrive around 10:30 AM. Card only at the parking machine.

    → 55 min drive
  • 11:30 AMGeysir geothermal field, free

    Strokkur erupts every six to ten minutes. Twenty-five minutes is genuinely enough time here.

    → 12 min drive
  • 01:00 PMGullfoss and lunch at the cafe, $24

    Lower platform first for scale, upper viewpoint second for the canyon. Waterproof shell, not an umbrella.

    → 45 min drive
  • 03:30 PMSecret Lagoon at Fludir, $32

    Smaller, older and much less polished than the Blue Lagoon. Many travelers prefer it.

    → 55 min drive
  • 06:30 PMCheck in near Hella, dinner in, $38

    Route 1 guesthouses cost less than Selfoss and put you ahead for the south coast tomorrow.

Driving: 168 milesWalking: 3.1 milesPace: moderate
Day 3

Waterfalls and black sand

Est. $171 / person
  • 08:15 AMSeljalandsfoss, $7 parking

    The path behind the falls soaks you completely. Rain pants, not just a jacket. Gljufrabui is a five-minute walk north and half as busy.

    → 25 min drive
  • 10:00 AMSkogafoss, free

    Climb the staircase to the upper viewpoint. You do not need the full Fimmvorduhals trail to get the payoff.

    → 20 min drive
  • 11:30 AMSolheimasandur plane wreck, optional

    A flat 2.5 mile walk each way with nothing to see on the way. Skip it unless you specifically want the photo.

    → 25 min drive
  • 12:15 PMLunch and fuel in Vik, $26

    Last dependable groceries before the sand plains east of town. Fill the tank here.

    → 10 min drive
  • 02:00 PMReynisfjara black sand beach, free

    Stay well back from the waterline. Sneaker waves here have killed visitors. Read the danger sign at the entrance.

    → 20 min drive
  • 08:30 PMAurora check from the guesthouse

    September through March only. Check the Icelandic Met Office aurora forecast before losing sleep over it.

Driving: 134 milesWalking: 4.6 milesPace: moderate
Day 4

Glacier lagoon and Diamond Beach

Est. $212 / person
  • 07:30 AMLeave Vik heading east

    The longest single stretch of the week. Coffee before you go, because options are thin for the first hour.

    → 1 hr 20 min drive
  • 09:00 AMFjadrargljufur canyon, free

    A short rim walk above a moss-lined canyon. The trail closes periodically for erosion recovery, so check before detouring.

    → 1 hr 40 min drive
  • 11:30 AMJokulsarlon glacier lagoon, free

    Icebergs calve off Breidamerkurjokull and drift to the sea. Zodiac boat tours run roughly $110 if you want to be among them.

    → 5 min walk
  • 01:30 PMDiamond Beach, free

    Ice chunks washed back onto black sand. Best in low sun, so morning or late evening beats midday.

    → 15 min drive
  • 03:00 PMLate lunch in Hofn, $32

    Langoustine is the local specialty and the town is built around it.

    → 50 min drive back west
  • 06:00 PMCheck in near Skaftafell, $58 dinner

    Book this one early. There are very few beds between Vik and Hofn.

Driving: 236 milesWalking: 2.8 milesPace: full
Day 5

Skaftafell, then the long drive west

Est. $168 / person
  • 08:00 AMSvartifoss hike, Skaftafell, $8 parking

    Three miles round trip with a steady climb. Basalt columns behind a narrow falls. Two hours at an easy pace.

    → 10 min drive
  • 11:00 AMGlacier walk on Falljokull, optional, $140

    Guided only, crampons provided. The one paid activity we think earns its price on this route.

    → 2 hr 30 min drive
  • 03:00 PMCoffee stop in Vik, $12

    Break the drive here rather than pushing through. Fatigue on Route 1 is a real hazard.

    → 2 hr 15 min drive
  • 06:30 PMCheck in at Borgarnes, $46 dinner

    An hour north of Reykjavik and the right base for the Snaefellsnes day tomorrow.

Driving: 292 milesWalking: 3.4 milesPace: driving day
Day 6

Snaefellsnes peninsula loop

Est. $142 / person
  • 08:30 AMLeave Borgarnes

    Often called Iceland in miniature: lava fields, black beaches, a glacier and fishing villages in one loop.

    → 1 hr 10 min drive
  • 10:00 AMKirkjufell and Kirkjufellsfoss, free

    The most photographed mountain in the country. Fifteen minutes, then move on.

    → 35 min drive
  • 11:30 AMDjupalonssandur black pebble beach, free

    Shipwreck remains left in place as a memorial. Do not move or take the metal.

    → 25 min drive
  • 01:00 PMLunch in Arnarstapi, $28

    Then the clifftop walk to Hellnar, about a mile each way along the coast.

    → 20 min drive
  • 04:00 PMYtri Tunga seal beach, free

    Seals haul out here reliably in summer. Watch from the marked area and stay 160 feet back.

    → 2 hr drive
  • 07:30 PMCheck in in Reykjavik, $54 dinner

    Last night in the city. Return the cooler contents to a grocery store bag and repack tonight, not tomorrow.

Driving: 218 milesWalking: 4.1 milesPace: moderate
Day 7

Reykjavik and departure

Est. $96 / person
  • 09:00 AMBreakfast and the harbor walk, $18

    Grandi is the working harbor district and has the best bakery in the city.

    → 12 min walk
  • 10:30 AMHarpa concert hall, free to enter

    Worth twenty minutes for the glass facade even if you are not seeing a performance.

    → 8 min walk
  • 11:30 AMLast shopping on Skolavordustigur, $40

    Wool goods and skyr to take home. Keep the receipts for the VAT refund desk at the airport.

    → 55 min drive to KEF
  • 02:00 PMReturn the rental car

    Photograph the car from all four sides before handing over the keys. Refuel within six miles of the airport.

    → 10 min shuttle
  • 03:30 PMCheck in for the afternoon departure

    Most US-bound flights leave between 3:00 PM and 5:30 PM and land the same evening.

Driving: 62 milesWalking: 3.2 milesPace: easy

How to adapt this

Only have five days? Cut days four and five entirely. Turn around at Vik and use the extra time for a second Reykjavik night. You lose the glacier lagoon, which is genuinely the best single sight on the route, but you gain a trip that does not feel like a rally stage.

Have ten days? Continue east from Hofn and complete the full Ring Road counterclockwise through Egilsstadir, Myvatn and Akureyri. That is the trip we would take if the PTO allowed.

Traveling in winter? Cut the Snaefellsnes day. Daylight between November and February is roughly 4 to 6 hours, and the peninsula loop in the dark is pointless and slightly dangerous.

Traveling with kids? Add a swimming pool stop most afternoons. Every Icelandic town has a geothermal public pool with slides, admission is $5 to $9, and it is the best-value thing in the country.
