Affiliate Disclosure

Last updated 08/01/2026. Written to comply with the Federal Trade Commission's Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising.

The short version: some links on this site earn Driftline a commission if you book through them. The price you pay is exactly the same. A commission never changes which places we recommend, what we say about them, or the order they appear in.

1. What an affiliate link is

An affiliate link is a normal link with a tracking parameter attached. If you click it and then make a booking, the provider records that the referral came from us and pays us a percentage of their own revenue on that transaction. The provider absorbs that cost out of their margin. It is not added to your price, and you would not pay less by navigating to the same page directly.

2. Where affiliate links appear on this site

  • Where to stay sections in destination guides, when we link to a booking platform.
  • Car rental mentions in road trip guides and itineraries.
  • Tour and activity links, such as a guided glacier walk or a boat trip, where a bookable operator exists.
  • Flight search links on some destination pages.
  • Travel insurance referrals, where a licensed partner pays a referral fee if you buy a policy.

Each of these carries a visible disclosure at the point of use, in the same section as the link, not buried at the bottom of the page. Look for a line that begins Affiliate disclosure.

3. Where affiliate links never appear

  • Inside the trip planner itself.
  • On any agent or white-label account. Affiliate links are switched off entirely for travel professionals and their clients.
  • In safety sections, entry requirement sections, or anywhere we link to a government source.
  • In custom itineraries produced by our paid service. That fee is flat precisely so that nobody on the team benefits from steering you toward an expensive option.
  • On any legal page, including this one.

4. What a commission does not do

This is the part that matters, so we will be specific.

  • It does not decide what we recommend. Neighborhoods, hotels, tours and restaurants are chosen by the editorial team on the basis of whether they are good and whether they fit the itinerary.
  • It does not change the order of anything. Where-to-stay sections are ordered by location and price band, never by commission rate.
  • It does not buy a mention. No property, operator or destination marketing organization can pay to appear in a Driftline guide. We have declined this repeatedly.
  • It does not change what we say. If a place is overpriced or overrated, the guide says so, whether or not a link to it earns us anything.
  • It does not affect our cost bands. Those come from research on actual market rates and are checked twice a year.

Several of the destinations we cover most heavily have poor affiliate economics. We cover them anyway, because our readers ask for them.

5. What we actually earn

Typical commission rates in travel affiliate programs run from roughly 3 to 8 percent of the supplier's revenue on a hotel booking, a few dollars per car rental day, and 5 to 10 percent on tours. Flight commissions are very small, often under one percent, which is why the airline industry generally does not fund travel content.

Across our business, affiliate commissions are a minority of total revenue and a smaller share every year. Pro subscriptions are the largest line. We prefer it that way, because subscriber money is the only money not tied to you buying something. Our full revenue breakdown is described in plain English on the How It Works page and the About page.

6. Advertising is separate

Some pages display advertising in clearly labeled slots. Advertising is sold through third-party networks and has no relationship to our editorial content. An advertiser cannot influence a guide and cannot buy a mention. Ad slots are visually distinct from real content, never sit above the main content of a page, and never appear on legal pages or the error page. Details on how advertising cookies work are in our Cookie Policy.

7. Sponsored content

We currently publish no sponsored posts, paid destination features or gifted-trip coverage. If that ever changes, any such content will be labeled Sponsored at the top of the page and in every listing that links to it, in plain language, and it will never be presented as an editorial recommendation.

We do occasionally accept press access, for example a media rate on a museum ticket. Where that happens and it is material to the coverage, we say so in the piece.

8. How to avoid affiliate links entirely

If you would rather not use them, you do not have to. Read the recommendation, note the property or operator name, and search for it directly. You will pay the same and we will earn nothing. Nothing on this site is hidden behind an affiliate click, and we do not make our guides harder to use for people who prefer to book independently.

9. FTC compliance

The Federal Trade Commission requires that material connections between a publisher and a product or service be disclosed clearly and conspicuously, close to the recommendation, in language an ordinary reader understands. Our approach is:

  • A disclosure line inside the same section as the affiliate link, before the link where practical.
  • A link to this page from the footer of every page on the site.
  • Plain language rather than legal boilerplate.
  • No disclosure hidden behind a hover, an expandable section, or a page that requires scrolling past the link to find.

10. Questions and complaints

If you think a recommendation on this site looks like it was influenced by a commission, tell us. We treat that as a bug in our editorial process rather than a complaint to be managed. Email hello@driftline.travel with the page and what looked wrong.
